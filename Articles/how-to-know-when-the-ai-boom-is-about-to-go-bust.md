# How to Know When the AI Boom Is About to Go Bust

- **Publication:** The Wall Street Journal — Heard on the Street
- **Author:** Jonathan Weil
- **Published:** September 27, 2026, at 5:30 a.m. EDT
- **Content type:** Analysis/outlook
- **Subject:** Funding gaps as a warning sign for the AI investment boom
- **Article:** [Read in Apple News](https://apple.news/A7kk2fiafTvSCqPHumTN9gA)
- **Topics:** Artificial intelligence, Market outlook, Capital markets, Investment risk, Data centers

## Summary

Jonathan Weil argues that the clearest warning of an AI bust would be major companies losing access to fresh capital before becoming self-sustaining. He cites Pets.com’s collapse in 2000 and Freddie Mac’s difficulty replenishing capital in 2007 as examples of financing constraints exposing fragile businesses. In his September 2026 assessment, postponed AI-related IPOs suggest nervousness but do not establish that funding is drying up, because private investment remains available. Continued backing for OpenAI also supports partners such as Oracle, whose borrowing and data-center spending depend on anticipated customer revenue. A funding shortfall at a major AI developer could therefore reverse momentum across the sector. However, private developers’ limited financial disclosure makes their resilience difficult to judge, and Weil offers neither a reliable timetable for a bust nor evidence that the decisive financing cutoff has already arrived.

## Key details

- **The warning to watch:** A company still needs outside money to cover losses but can no longer raise it. Weil treats that financing gap as the critical turning point.
- **Historical evidence:** Pets.com failed after investor funding disappeared. Freddie Mac’s $6 billion preferred-stock sale in November 2007 illustrated the difficulty of obtaining common equity that could better absorb mortgage losses.
- **The qualification:** The article reports postponed or paused public offerings involving OpenAI, Anthropic and Holtec Nuclear, but says private financing still appears available. It also notes that Holtec already has profitable, established nuclear operations.
- **Potential spillover:** Weil connects OpenAI’s continued financing to Oracle’s expected revenue and debt-funded data-center construction. Trouble raising money at one major developer could affect businesses investing around it.
- **Limits of the argument:** The article identifies a conditional financial vulnerability. It does not establish when a funding cutoff will occur or provide enough public financial information to determine the developers’ remaining cash runway.
