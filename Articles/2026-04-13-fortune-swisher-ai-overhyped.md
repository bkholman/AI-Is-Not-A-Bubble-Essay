## Summary

Fortune reports Kara Swisher’s argument that dissatisfaction with artificial experiences could limit AI’s impact despite the industry’s ambitions. She sees growing demand for authentic human connection, especially among younger people. The article cites a Walton Family Foundation survey showing declining optimism about AI among Gen Z and rising concern about its workplace risks. It connects that sentiment with layoffs, uncertainty about entry-level careers, and interest in less easily automated work. The author also raises a potential economic contradiction: replacing workers could weaken demand for the products companies sell. These are arguments about acceptance and employment, not proof that AI lacks useful applications; the article acknowledges that layoffs attributed to AI can also reflect other business decisions.
