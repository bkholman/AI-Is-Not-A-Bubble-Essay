## Summary

Solo Ceesay argues that AI promotion has outrun demonstrated demand, profitability, and productivity. He contrasts corporate layoffs and heavy investment with a cited survey of executives reporting limited effects from AI, and contends that financial-market concentration gives powerful institutions incentives to sustain the narrative. He also presents government adoption and competition with China as forces driving investment regardless of near-term consumer demand. His broader concern is that displacement of higher-income workers could weaken spending in an already unequal economy. The commentary makes sweeping claims about market dependence and the motives behind adoption, rather than establishing a causal forecast of collapse. Its conclusion leaves both an AI-driven golden age and a damaging outcome possible, despite the headline’s categorical framing.
