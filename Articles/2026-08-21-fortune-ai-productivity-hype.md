## Summary

Venture investor Sanjot Malhi argues that the crowded market for AI productivity tools has attracted more funding than many businesses can justify. He questions whether easily replicated applications can retain customers and margins as model providers add similar features and open-source alternatives improve. He favors businesses with proprietary data, deeply embedded workflows, and opportunities to automate work more comprehensively. This is a critique of business durability and investment allocation, not a rejection of AI’s potential: Malhi recognizes productivity gains and remains optimistic about applications in science, medicine, and the physical world. His commentary also discusses his firm’s investment thesis and portfolio, a commercial perspective readers should consider when weighing the argument.
