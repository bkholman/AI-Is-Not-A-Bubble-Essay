# BYU undergraduate years: the only laptop in history class

When I started at Brigham Young University as a freshman in 1988, I took a history class with no textbook. The professor lectured throughout the class, and the other students were trying to keep up on their yellow notepads.

I was the only person there with a laptop: a Tandy Model 102 portable computer that I had bought while I was still in high school. I was typing a hundred words a minute.

I could capture far more than I could with a pen, and classmates asked me for copies of my notes.

Those machines were used by some reporters, who could connect a modem and send their stories to a newspaper. I had brought mine to college because I could see how useful a portable computer would be.

In 1988, I already believed laptops were going to be part of the way people worked. That classroom is one of the clearest memories I have of seeing a change before it looked obvious to everyone else.
