# WebMiles: a real idea in the dot-com bust

[WebMiles](../Professional%20Background/03-webmiles.md) was my first executive job and my first venture-funded startup experience. We raised $40 million during the dot-com era to build a universal airline-miles loyalty program. Customers could earn miles by shopping with a family of merchants.

Our largest client was Kroger, accounting for about 70 percent of our revenue, and we were running a pilot with Ralphs. Customers could opt into WebMiles through their Ralphs loyalty cards. We saw what we called the loyalty effect: customers would pass a closer grocery store to shop at the one where they earned the rewards.

There was real value in that idea. We had venture backing, involvement from Silicon Valley Bank, and a former American Express executive recruited as CEO. We also wasted money. We spent millions on “rent-a-relative” commercials that, in my view, had little to do with the business. We lived through some of the same excess that people associate with Pets.com.

Then September 11 happened. Kroger became nervous about promoting travel and tourism. There was fear that people might not travel again, and our biggest customer was not ready to proceed.

I believed we could bridge that interruption with more funding and turn WebMiles into a successful long-term business. Our investors did not provide that bridge, and we shut down. We became another casualty of the dot-com period.

That is why the comparison with today’s AI boom is personal to me. I have seen a company with a useful idea fail amid waste, customer concentration, a sudden shock, and a loss of funding. I still believe the idea had value.
